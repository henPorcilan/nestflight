# Nestflight — Parent Discovery Interview Script

Target: 30–50 parents of school-age kids. Goal: validate the problem and willingness to pay *before* building further — not to pitch the product. Follows Mom Test principles: ask about their past behavior and actual money/time spent, not opinions about your idea.

**Length:** 20 minutes. **Format:** 1:1, video or phone.

---

## 0. Before you start

- Don't describe Nestflight until the very end (Section 5). If they ask what you're building, say "I'll tell you at the end — I want to hear how you do this today first."
- Write down what they *did*, not what they *say they'd do*. "I'd love an app like that" is not signal. "I spent 3 hours last Tuesday comparing flight dates" is signal.

---

## 1. Warm-up / context (2 min)

1. How many kids do you have, and what ages/grades?
2. Are they all in the same school, or different schools/districts?
3. Roughly how many trips — of any size — did your family take last year?

---

## 2. Current behavior: how they plan today (7 min)

4. Walk me through the last trip you planned, start to finish. Where did you start?
5. How did you decide *when* to go? (Listen for: did school calendars drive the date, or did the date get picked first and then they worked around school?)
6. Did you ever have to check multiple kids' school calendars against each other? What was that like?
7. Tell me about a time a trip's timing didn't line up well with school — a make-up day, a random early dismissal, missed days. What happened?
8. What tools, sites, or spreadsheets did you actually use? (Listen for: Google Flights, a shared family calendar, a Notes app, nothing at all.)
9. How much time would you guess you spent researching before booking that trip?

---

## 3. Money and deals (5 min)

10. Tell me about the last time you got a genuinely good flight or hotel deal. How did you find it?
11. Tell me about a time you found out about a deal *too late* — after it expired or sold out.
12. Do you or your partner have airline miles, hotel points, or credit card points sitting around? Roughly how much, if you know?
13. Have you ever used those points for a trip? What made that easy or hard?
14. Walk me through how you decided on your last trip's budget. Did points or a card sign-up bonus factor in?

---

## 4. Willingness to pay (4 min)

Ask this even if the product isn't built — the specificity of the answer is the signal, not politeness.

15. If a tool did all of this automatically — pulled in your kids' school calendars, told you the best-timed windows, and flagged deals and point redemptions for those exact dates — what would that be worth to you, per year?
16. Would you rather pay a flat subscription for that, or have it be free and make money by referring you to book the flight/hotel (at no extra cost to you)?
17. Is there a travel newsletter, app, or subscription you already pay for? What is it, and what do you get from it?

---

## 5. Reveal and close (2 min)

Now describe Nestflight in 2–3 sentences using their own language from the interview where possible. Then ask:

18. What's the one thing that would make you *not* trust or use something like this?
19. Who else do you know who juggles multiple kids' school schedules and travel planning? Can you introduce me?

Thank them. Add them to the waitlist if they're interested.

---

## After every interview: fill this in immediately

- **Problem confirmed?** (Y/N) — did they describe real pain, unprompted, in Section 2–3?
- **Current workaround:** ___________
- **Points/miles held?** ___________ (rough amount + programs)
- **Willingness-to-pay signal:** ___________ (specific number or "free only")
- **Best quote:** ___________
- **Referral given?** (Y/N)

---

## What "good" looks like after 30–50 interviews

- A majority describe school-calendar-driven trip planning as genuinely annoying, unprompted, before you mention it.
- At least a third have missed a deal or booked in the wrong season and can point to a specific instance.
- A meaningful share (not necessarily majority) give a real number for willingness to pay, or clearly prefer the affiliate/referral model over a subscription.
- If none of this shows up, the wedge is weaker than assumed — revisit before building further.
